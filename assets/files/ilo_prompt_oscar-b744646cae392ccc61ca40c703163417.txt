2. Rules for Creating ILOs
Follow these rules strictly:

1. ILO Categories
You must create ILOs under all four categories:

- Disciplinary Knowledge
- Disciplinary Skills
- Generic Skills
- Values and Attitudes

2. Use Bloom's Taxonomy

- Start each ILO with a clear action verb from Bloom's Taxonomy.
- For Disciplinary Knowledge, prioritize lower‑order thinking verbs appropriate to the grade level.
- For Disciplinary Skills, move to higher‑order thinking verbs, again calibrated to the grade level.
- For Generic Skills and Values and Attitudes, choose suitable verbs that still make the outcome observable and assessable .


3. Quality Criteria (from the ILO Guidelines)
Every ILO must be:
- Student‑centred
- Measurable
- Specific
- Appropriate in difficulty

4. Cognitive Progression (Low → High)

Ensure an overall progression from lower‑order cognitive demand (mainly in Disciplinary Knowledge) to higher‑order cognitive demand (especially in Disciplinary Skills).
Within each category, order the ILOs roughly from simpler to more complex thinking, taking into account the grade level and total teaching time.


5. Alignment with Course Information

ILOs must align closely with:
- the course topic and description,
- the Key Learning Area,
- the grade level,
- the total number of lessons and duration per lesson.

Do not propose outcomes that exceed what is realistically achievable within the given time and grade level.

6. Number of ILOs

Suggest a reasonable number of ILOs for each category so that the total is achievable within the course (for example, about 4 ILOs per category, unless the Course Information indicates otherwise).

3. Output Format
Start with a brief, 2–3 sentence summary explaining:

the assumed level of cognitive demand (based on the grade level), and
how the ILOs progress from lower‑order to higher‑order thinking.
